Privacy Policy — TimeFrame

Last updated: November 19, 2026

TimeFrame (www.timeframecalender.com, "the app") helps you and your group find shared free time. It combines when you're busy, from calendars you connect and times you enter by hand, and suggests meeting times that work for everyone. This page explains exactly what the app reads, what it stores, and how it's used.

The short version

  • TimeFrame only needs to know when you're busy, never what you're doing. Event titles, descriptions, locations and attendees are never stored.
  • Calendar access is read-only and used only to show your availability inside the app.
  • We don't sell your data, show ads, or share it with anyone outside the services listed below.

Google Calendar

When you continue with Google, TimeFrame asks for read-only access to your calendars (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.readonly). It reads events from the calendars you have turned on in Google Calendar (holiday and birthday calendars are skipped), for the past week and the next year. From each event it requests only:

  • the start and end time (and the event's time zone),
  • whether the event is marked "Free" or is a working-location marker (those are ignored), and
  • your own RSVP, so invitations you declined don't count as busy.

It does not request event titles, descriptions, locations, attachments or other attendees. It also reads your Google account email address, which becomes your TimeFrame account. Google access is used once, at the moment you connect or reconnect, to import busy times; TimeFrame does not keep a Google access or refresh token.

Google API Services User Data Policy (Limited Use)

TimeFrame's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically, Google user data is used only to provide and improve TimeFrame's availability features you can see in the app; it is not transferred to others except as needed to provide those features (the hosting providers below), to comply with law, or as part of a merger or acquisition; it is never used for advertising; it is never sold; and no person reads it, except with your permission, for security purposes, or to comply with law. It is not used to train AI or machine-learning models.

Apple Calendar, Notion and calendar files

  • Apple Calendar (iCloud): you enter your Apple ID email and an app-specific password. They are used once to read busy times from your iCloud calendars and are never stored. You can revoke the app-specific password at any time at account.apple.com.
  • Notion: if you connect Notion, it asks you which pages and databases to share. TimeFrame reads date values from the databases you share and turns timed entries into busy time. The Notion access token is used once and not stored.
  • .ics files: if you upload calendar files, only the event times are kept.

What we store

  • Your email address (your account), a display name if you set one, and, for email accounts only, a securely hashed password
  • Busy time ranges (start, end, time zone, and which source they came from); never titles or other event details
  • Times you block off by hand (again, only the times)
  • Groups you create or join, and which calendar sources you've connected

Group sharing

If you create or join a group with a shared link, the other members of that group can see your busy time ranges (as blocks with your display name) so the app can find a time that works for everyone. They never see what your events are. Only share a group link with people you're comfortable seeing when you're busy.

Services that process data for us

  • Vercel hosts the website
  • Render runs the app's server
  • Supabase hosts the database
  • Google, Apple and Notion, only when you choose to connect them

Browser storage

The app keeps your sign-in session and a cached copy of your schedule in your browser's local storage so pages load quickly between visits. It stays on your device.

TimeFrame Chrome extension

The TimeFrame extension shows your best free times in a small toolbar popup. It uses the same account as this website: when you visit timeframecalender.com while signed in, the extension reads only your TimeFrame sign-in token, your display settings (day hours, meeting length, preferred times) and your user ID from this site, and keeps them in the extension's own storage on your device, together with the group you picked. It reads nothing on any other website, does not collect browsing history, and talks only to the TimeFrame API (calender-app-mm4q.onrender.com) to load your free times and groups. Nothing is sold or shared. Removing the extension, or choosing Sign out in it, deletes what it stored.

Your choices and deleting your data

  • Disconnect: each calendar card has a Disconnect button that removes that source's imported busy times; "Logout / Disconnect Google" removes your Google-imported times.
  • Clear Schedule removes the cached copy in your browser.
  • Delete everything: email sam.esebor@gmail.com from your account's address and we'll delete your account and all its data within 30 days.
  • You can also remove TimeFrame's access to Google at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Security

Data is sent over HTTPS. Every request is tied to a signed session, so no one can read your schedule by guessing a link or an email address. Passwords are stored only as salted hashes.

Children's privacy

TimeFrame is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change too. Significant changes will be noted in the app.
